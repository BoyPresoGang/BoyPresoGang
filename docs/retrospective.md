# Team Retrospective

## What Went Well

At the beginning of the project, our team needed time to become familiar with GitHub, the development workflow, and the expectations of working on a shared project. As the weeks progressed, we became more comfortable with branches, commits, pull requests, merging, issue tracking, and organizing our work through the GitHub repository and project board.

One of the most valuable parts of the project was experiencing how software development works as a team rather than as an individual task. We learned that working on a shared codebase requires awareness of other members' work because changes can conflict, affect existing functionality, or require coordination before they can be merged.

The project also became an important learning experience beyond simply completing school requirements. It gave us an introduction to practices that are relevant to actual software development, particularly version control, collaboration, code review, task organization, and maintaining a shared repository.

We also became more conscious of how AI should be used during development. Rather than simply asking an AI tool to complete tasks, we learned to provide clear instructions, review its output, identify mistakes, and verify whether the result actually matched the requirements. Although we still encountered mistakes and had areas where we could improve, the project helped us understand that AI is a tool that still requires human judgment and verification.

Another positive outcome was learning how to organize a GitHub repository more effectively. Keeping related documentation, tasks, and project materials organized made the repository easier to navigate and made our work easier to track throughout the project.

## What Didn't Go Well

Our biggest difficulty at the beginning was getting accustomed to the workflow. We initially had limited experience with GitHub-based collaboration and did not immediately understand the importance of maintaining individual contributions and keeping each member involved in the development process.

There were also situations where coordination could have been better. Working on the same project meant that changes sometimes needed to be coordinated to avoid conflicts or duplicated work. This showed us that completing a task individually is not always enough; team members also need to communicate about what they are working on.

Another challenge was translating early ideas and wireframes into a clear final implementation. Some of the details represented in our initial designs, such as certain UI fields and interface elements, were not fully reflected in the final system. This taught us that having a general idea of a system is not enough; the expected outcome needs to be clearly considered and documented before implementation.

The project also exposed areas where our technical independence still needs improvement. There were situations, particularly during AI-restricted activities, where using AI had become a significant part of our workflow. This made us realize that we need to continue strengthening our understanding of the underlying concepts so that we can solve problems even without AI assistance.

## What We Would Change Next Time

If we were to start the project again, we would spend more time establishing the expected outcome before beginning implementation. This would include reviewing the requirements carefully and making sure that our wireframes, planned features, database structure, and actual implementation are aligned.

We would also establish clearer contribution expectations from the beginning. Each member should have meaningful responsibilities and should actively contribute rather than waiting until the end of a task or deadline. Tasks should be distributed fairly while also considering dependencies between team members.

We would improve our time management as well. Tasks should be completed within their intended schedule instead of being delayed until close to the deadline. This would give the team more time to review work, resolve conflicts, test changes, and provide assistance when necessary.

We would also place greater emphasis on technical independence. AI can help explain concepts, suggest solutions, and accelerate development, but it should not replace understanding. We would continue using AI as a development aid while making sure that every team member can explain, review, and verify the work being produced.

Finally, we would encourage more frequent second opinions. Before finalizing an important decision or implementation, another team member should have the opportunity to review it. This can help identify mistakes that may not be obvious to the person who created the work.

## Lessons Learned

### 1. Teamwork Requires Active Contribution

Working on one project means that every member's contribution matters. Team members should not wait to be told what to do. When assigned a task, the expectation should be to take initiative, complete it properly, and communicate when assistance is needed.

### 2. Help Teammates Without Creating Dependency

Team members should support one another when someone is struggling, but assistance should help the person learn rather than make them dependent on another member. The goal is for everyone to become more capable throughout the project.

### 3. Meet Deadlines

A task that is delayed can affect other members whose work depends on it. Completing assigned work on time gives the team enough opportunity to review, integrate, test, and improve the project.

### 4. Follow Requirements Thoroughly

Instructions should be read carefully and verified before implementation. AI tools can help interpret requirements and assist with development, but the team remains responsible for fact-checking the output and making sure the result actually satisfies the requirements.

### 5. Use AI With Judgment

AI can be extremely useful, but it should not be treated as a replacement for understanding. We learned to provide better instructions, review generated output, test the result, and correct mistakes rather than blindly accepting what an AI tool produces.

### 6. Communicate and Coordinate

Working on a shared repository requires awareness of what other members are doing. Branches, commits, pull requests, merges, and potential conflicts all require communication and coordination.

### 7. Have a Second Opinion

Another person's perspective can reveal problems that the original developer may overlook. Code, designs, and decisions benefit from review before they become part of the final system.

### 8. Be Flexible and Willing to Learn

The project required us to work with tools and practices that were initially unfamiliar. Being open to learning, listening to others, and trying different approaches helped us adapt as the project progressed.

### 9. Plan the Expected Outcome Before Implementation

A system should not only be functional; the team should have a clear understanding of what it is supposed to look like and how it should behave. Better planning of wireframes, requirements, and expected outputs would have reduced differences between the original design and the final implementation.

### 10. Take Initiative

One of the strongest lessons from the project was the importance of initiative. A team works better when members actively look for ways to contribute instead of waiting for someone else to assign every action. Taking responsibility for assigned work also helps prevent one member from carrying too much of the project.

## Final Reflection

This project became one of the most significant learning experiences of the school year. It felt different from simply completing individual assignments because the work represented a continuous development process that lasted twelve weeks.

We learned that building software involves more than writing code. It requires planning, communication, version control, testing, reviewing other people's work, resolving conflicts, managing deadlines, and taking responsibility for the result.

Most importantly, the project gave us a clearer introduction to how collaborative software development works. We still have many areas to improve, particularly in technical independence and planning, but the experience gave us a stronger foundation for working on larger projects in the future.
